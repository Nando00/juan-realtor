Choosing an agent can feel strangely high-stakes. You are about to hand someone the keys to one of the biggest decisions of your life, often after a single phone call. It is worth slowing down for that part.

Most guides on how to choose a realtor in Houston are written to sell you on one particular agent. This one is meant to help you choose well, even if that means choosing someone else. Whether you are buying or selling, the goal is the same: find a person who listens, explains clearly, and works for your interests.

## Start with what you need, not who you know

Before you call anyone, write down what you want help with. A first home in the Heights is a different job from selling a long-time family home in Memorial. A move across town on a tight timeline is different again.

A few notes make every conversation more useful:

- **Your goal:** buying, selling, or both, and roughly when.
- **Your areas:** the neighborhoods or commutes you care about.
- **Your worries:** budget, timing, a home that needs work, or a house you need to sell first.
- **How you like to communicate:** calls, texts, email, and how often.

Friends and family referrals are a fine place to start. Just treat them as a short list, not a decision. The agent who was perfect for your cousin may not be the right fit for you.

## How to find a good real estate agent in Houston

Houston is big, and local knowledge matters. Look for agents who spend real time in the areas you are considering, whether that is Montrose, the Heights, Memorial, or somewhere else entirely.

Talk to two or three people. That is usually enough to compare styles without turning the search into a second job. Pay attention to how each one responds before you have signed anything. The way an agent treats you when they are trying to earn your trust is a good preview of how they will treat you later.

> A good first conversation should feel like the agent is learning about you, not performing for you.

## How to interview a real estate agent

An interview does not need to be formal. A coffee or a 20-minute call works. What matters is that you ask open questions and listen to how the answers are given, not just what they are.

### Questions to ask a realtor before hiring

These questions work for buyers and sellers alike:

1. **What does a typical week look like when you are working with a client like me?** You are listening for specifics: how often you will hear from them, and who you will actually be talking to.
2. **Who else will be involved?** Some agents work alone, some in teams. Either can work well. You just want to know who handles showings, paperwork, and questions.
3. **What do you know about the areas I am considering?** Ask about things that matter to your life, like commutes, flood history, or how homes in that area tend to sell.
4. **How do you handle disagreements or bad news?** Every transaction has a hard moment. You want someone who tells you the truth calmly and early.
5. **What will you need from me, and when?** Good agents set expectations on both sides.
6. **How are you paid, and what does that cover?** Ask this plainly. A clear answer is a good sign.

### Extra questions if you are buying

- How do you help buyers decide what to offer?
- What happens if I want to stop working together?
- Will you show me homes you are not paid well on? A good buyer's agent should say yes without hesitating.

### Extra questions if you are selling

- How would you price my home, and what would you use to support that number?
- How will you market it, and what will I be paying for?
- How long is the listing agreement, and how can it end?

## Texas specifics worth checking

Texas has its own rules, and a few minutes of checking can save you a lot of worry later.

### Verify the license with TREC

Anyone who represents you in a Texas real estate transaction should hold an active license with the Texas Real Estate Commission. You can look up any agent or brokerage by name or license number with the [TREC License Holder Search](https://www.trec.texas.gov/apps/license-holder-search/). Confirm the license is active and note the sponsoring broker.

### Expect the Information About Brokerage Services notice

Texas law requires a license holder to give you a written notice about agency, called **Information About Brokerage Services** (often shortened to IABS), at the first substantive conversation about a specific property. It explains the ways a brokerage can work with you and who it represents. TREC publishes the official form, and agents' websites are required to link to it. You can read more on [TREC's IABS page](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/information-about-brokerage-services).

Read it. It is short, and it is one of the clearest explanations of agency you will find. If an agent never brings it up, that is worth asking about.

### Buyers sign a written agreement before touring

Since August 17, 2024, following the National Association of REALTORS® settlement, buyers working with many agents are asked to sign a written buyer agreement before touring a home, in person or virtually. NAR's [consumer guide to written buyer agreements](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-written-buyer-agreements) explains what that means.

Texas went further. Starting January 1, 2026, Texas law requires a written agreement with a prospective buyer of residential property before an agent shows any home, or before presenting an offer if no home is shown. TREC summarizes the change in [What Changes in 2026 About Buyer/Tenant Representation in Texas](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/what-changes-2026-about-buyertenant-representation-texas). That agreement must state:

- The services the agent will provide.
- When the agreement ends.
- Whether it is exclusive or non-exclusive.
- Whether the agent represents you, or only shows homes without representing you.
- How much the broker will be paid, and how that is determined.

It must also say, in conspicuous language, that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable.

### Commissions are negotiable

That last point deserves its own line. How much an agent is paid, and who pays it, is something you discuss and agree on. You can still ask for the seller to contribute toward your agent's pay as part of an offer. Do not sign anything you do not understand, and do not feel rushed.

## Signs you have found the right fit

After your conversations, notice how you feel. The right agent usually leaves you feeling calmer, not more pressured.

| Good signs | Worth a second look |
| --- | --- |
| Asks about your life before talking about listings | Leads with promises about price or speed |
| Explains agreements and fees without being asked twice | Gets vague when you ask how they are paid |
| Is honest about tradeoffs in a neighborhood | Says every home is a great deal |
| Answers questions in plain language | Hurries you toward signing |

## FAQ

### How do I choose a realtor in Houston?

Interview two or three agents who know the neighborhoods you care about, and ask how they communicate, how they are paid, and who will handle your day-to-day questions. Verify each license on the TREC License Holder Search. Choose the person who listens best and explains things most clearly.

### What questions should I ask a real estate agent before hiring them?

Ask what working together looks like week to week, who else will be involved, what they know about your target areas, and how they handle bad news. Always ask how they are paid and what that covers. Clear, specific answers are a good sign.

### Do I need a buyer's agent in Houston?

You are not required to have one, but a Houston buyer's agent represents your interests in pricing, negotiation, and paperwork. In Texas, any agent who shows you homes must have a written agreement with you first, and that agreement states whether they represent you. Read it carefully so you know what you are getting.

### Do I have to sign an agreement before touring a home in Texas?

Yes, if an agent is showing you the home. Since January 1, 2026, Texas law requires a written agreement before an agent shows a buyer residential property. Visiting an open house on your own or simply asking an agent about their services is different.

### Are real estate commissions negotiable in Texas?

Yes. Texas buyer agreements must state, in conspicuous language, that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. The same is true when you talk with a listing agent about selling.

### How do I check if a Texas realtor is licensed?

Use the TREC License Holder Search on the Texas Real Estate Commission website. Search by name or license number to confirm the license is active and see the sponsoring broker.
