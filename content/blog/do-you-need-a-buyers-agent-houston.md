If you have started looking at homes lately, you may have noticed something new. Before an agent will open the front door, they ask you to sign an agreement. That is often when the question really lands: do I need a buyer's agent in Houston, and what am I agreeing to?

The official explanations are accurate, but they describe rules rather than help you decide. This guide walks through what changed, who pays the buyer's agent in Texas, and how to think about the agreement in front of you, in plain English.

One note before we start: this is general information, not legal advice. If you have a legal question about a specific agreement, a real estate attorney is the right person to ask.

## What changed, and why you are signing something now

### The NAR settlement changes (August 17, 2024)

After the National Association of REALTORS® settled commission litigation, practice changes took effect on August 17, 2024. According to NAR's page on [what the settlement means for buyers and sellers](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers):

- Agents who use an MLS must have a written agreement with a buyer before touring a home, in person or by live virtual tour.
- Offers of compensation to buyer's agents are no longer allowed on the MLS. Sellers can still offer compensation off the MLS, and they can still offer buyer concessions, like help with closing costs, on the MLS.
- The agreement must state the agent's pay in clear terms, and the agent cannot accept more from any source than it allows.
- Fees are fully negotiable and not set by law.

### The Texas law change (January 1, 2026)

Texas then wrote a similar rule into state law. TREC's summary, [What Changes in 2026 About Buyer/Tenant Representation in Texas](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/what-changes-2026-about-buyertenant-representation-texas), explains that a license holder working with a residential buyer must have a written agreement before showing any home, or before presenting an offer if no home is shown.

For NAR settlement Houston home buyers, the result is simple: if a licensed agent is going to show you a home, expect paperwork first.

## Do you need a buyer's agent in Houston?

No law says you must hire one. The real question is whether you want someone whose job is to look out for you.

The [Information About Brokerage Services](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/information-about-brokerage-services) notice, the TREC form agents must give you, spells out the difference. A buyer's agent must put your interests above all others, including their own, tell you material information they learn about the property or the deal, answer your questions, and present your offers. A listing agent owes those duties to the seller.

That matters most when real money is on the line: deciding what to offer, reading inspection results, negotiating repairs, and keeping deadlines straight. If you are comfortable handling those yourself, or with an attorney, you may not need full representation. If you would rather have someone in your corner, that is what a buyer's agent is for.

## Representation or showing only: the two kinds of agreements

Since January 2026, Texas law allows two forms of written agreement with a buyer, according to TREC.

### A showing-only, non-representation agreement

An agent can show you a property without representing you. Under this kind of agreement, the agent:

- Cannot give you opinions or advice about the property or the deal.
- Cannot do other brokerage work for you.
- Can still share facts like the size, price, and terms of the property.

The agreement must be non-exclusive and cannot last more than 14 days.

### A buyer representation agreement

This is what most buyers mean by "my agent." The agent represents you and owes you the duties in the IABS notice. A representation agreement can still limit the services, for example to showings only, but it cannot waive the minimum duties the agent owes you.

Texas REALTORS® publishes two buyer representation forms its members use, a long form (TXR 1501) and a short form (TXR 1507). Its [legal FAQ on buyer/tenant representation agreements](https://www.texasrealestate.com/legal-and-ethics/legal-resources/legal-faqs/buyer-tenant-representation-agreements) notes that the short form can cover a market area, such as one property, a subdivision, a city, or a ZIP code, so you do not need a new agreement for every address.

## Who pays the buyer's agent in Texas?

Under your buyer agreement, you are responsible for paying your agent what it says. That does not always mean it comes out of your pocket. NAR's [consumer guide to written buyer agreements](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-written-buyer-agreements) says you can still request, negotiate for, and receive compensation for your agent from the seller or the seller's agent.

Your offer is where you sort that out:

- The seller agrees to pay some or all of your agent's fee.
- You ask the seller for a concession and use it toward your costs.
- You pay some or all of the fee yourself.

Read the payment section closely. Texas REALTORS® points out, for example, that under the long form a buyer may owe the broker's fee if the seller refuses or fails to pay it. Know what your agreement says before you sign.

### What is a buyer's agent fee in Houston?

There is no standard number. Both TREC and NAR are clear that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. Texas law requires the agreement to state the amount or rate of compensation and how it is determined, and to say in conspicuous language that it is negotiable.

Under NAR's rules, the fee can be a percentage, a flat fee, an hourly rate, or even $0, but it must be specific. Texas REALTORS® adds that it cannot be a range or change depending on who pays.

## Should you sign before touring homes in Houston?

If a licensed agent is showing you a home, some written agreement has to come first. But you have more choices than "sign everything" or "tour nothing."

- **Visit open houses on your own.** NAR notes you do not need an agreement to visit an open house on your own. One Texas nuance from TREC: if the host is not with the listing brokerage, they must give you the IABS and a written agreement before you view the home.
- **Start with a short agreement.** A short term, a narrow area, or a non-exclusive agreement lets you get to know an agent before committing further.
- **Interview first.** Asking an agent about their services does not require a signature. Our guide on [how to choose a realtor in Houston](/blog/how-to-choose-a-realtor-in-houston) covers what to ask.

## What to look at before you sign a buyer representation agreement in Houston

You do not need to be an expert to read it well. Focus on a few questions:

1. **How long does it last?**
2. **What area does it cover?** One home, a neighborhood, or the whole Houston area?
3. **Is it exclusive,** or can you work with other agents too?
4. **Does the agent represent me, or only show homes?**
5. **What exactly will they do?** Showings only, or offers, negotiation, and help through closing?
6. **How much, and who pays if the seller will not?**
7. **How can it end early?** NAR notes that you and your agent can agree to change it, and that the agreement may set conditions for exiting.

If something is unclear, ask for a plain-language explanation. For legal questions, a real estate attorney can review it before you sign.

> The right agreement is one you understand completely before you tour a single home.

## FAQ

### Do I need a buyer's agent to buy a house in Houston?

No. You can buy without one. A buyer's agent represents your interests in pricing, negotiation, and paperwork. Any agent who shows you a home must first have a written agreement with you that says whether they represent you.

### Who pays the buyer's agent in Texas now?

Your buyer agreement says what your agent is paid, and you are responsible for that amount. You can still ask the seller to pay some or all of it in your offer.

### What is the typical buyer's agent fee in Houston?

There is no set fee. Broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. Your agreement must state a specific amount or rate, so you know the number before you tour.

### Do I have to sign a buyer agreement before touring a house in Texas?

Yes, if a licensed agent is showing you the home. That has been Texas law since January 1, 2026. It can be a showing-only agreement of up to 14 days that does not make the agent your representative.

### Can I get out of a buyer representation agreement in Texas?

You and your agent can agree to change it, and the agreement may list its own exit terms. Read that section before you sign, and ask a real estate attorney about legal questions.
