Buying your first home is exciting, and a little overwhelming. Most of what you find about being a first-time home buyer in Houston covers one piece of the path, not the whole thing.

This guide walks through it in order: getting ready, finding out what help you might qualify for, choosing where to look, and what happens once you make an offer. It also answers a question we hear often: is a place like the Heights, Montrose, or Memorial realistic for a first purchase?

## Step 1: Get clear on your money first

Before the listing apps, start with your numbers:

- **A comfortable monthly payment**, including property taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues.
- **Your savings** for a down payment, closing costs, and a cushion after you move in.
- **Your credit and debts**, because lenders and assistance programs both look at how much of your income already goes to monthly payments.

Then talk to a lender, or two or three. A pre-approval tells you what a lender will lend. You decide what you are comfortable spending.

If you might use down payment assistance, take a homebuyer education course early. Several programs below require one from a HUD-approved or HUD-certified provider, and the City of Houston's program asks for an eight-hour course.

## Step 2: Find out which assistance programs fit you

Houston buyers can look at city, county, and statewide help. Rules and funding change, so treat the details below as a starting point and confirm current terms with each program or a participating lender.

### City of Houston Homebuyer Assistance Program (HAP)

The city's [Homebuyer Assistance Program](https://houstontx.gov/housing/hap.html) offers up to $75,000, based on financial need, to income-qualified first-time buyers. The city says you generally need to:

- Be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident (including any co-applicant and spouse).
- Be a first-time buyer, or not have owned a home in the last three years.
- Have household income at or below 80% of the Area Median Income.
- Buy a home that pays taxes to the City of Houston.

The help is a no-interest, forgivable loan, forgiven if you live in the home for eight years. Sell or move out sooner, and you repay a portion.

One important note: as of this writing, the HAP page says available funds have been used up and the city is not accepting or processing new applications until funding is replenished. Check with the city before you plan around it.

### Harris County programs

Buying outside Houston city limits? Harris County Housing and Community Development runs a [Down Payment Assistance Program](https://hcd.harriscountytx.gov/Resident-Programs/Homeownership-Programs/Down-Payment-Assistance) for first-time buyers in unincorporated parts of the county. It lists a maximum award of $40,000 and purchase price caps of $285,000 for an existing home and $296,000 for a new one, plus credit, debt, income, location, and flood-area rules. Right now, the county says it is only accepting applicants affected by Hurricane Beryl and the derecho, because it has reached capacity for everyone else.

The [Harris County Housing Finance Corporation](https://harriscountyhfc.org/homebuyer-programs/) is separate. It partners with the state housing agency, TDHCA, and says it offers down payment assistance that can be used only alongside TDHCA's mortgage program, subject to available funds.

### TDHCA My First Texas Home

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs runs [My First Texas Home](https://welcomehome.tdhca.texas.gov/programs) for first-time buyers and qualified veterans. Its [lender guide](https://welcomehome.tdhca.texas.gov/uploads/Lender-Guide.pdf) describes a 30-year mortgage with down payment and closing cost help of up to 5% of the loan, as either a deferred repayable second lien or a three-year forgivable one. You generally count as a first-time buyer if you have not owned and lived in a primary residence in the past three years. TDHCA also lists a 620 minimum credit score, a required HUD-certified education course, and income and price limits that vary by county.

### TSAHC Home Sweet Texas and Homes for Texas Heroes

The Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation offers [Home Sweet Texas](https://www.tsahc.org/program/home-sweet-texas-homes/) for low- and moderate-income buyers and [Homes for Texas Heroes](https://www.tsahc.org/program/homes-for-texas-heroes/) for teachers, police officers, firefighters, EMS, corrections officers, county jailers, and veterans. Both are open to first-time and repeat buyers and offer up to 5% of the loan amount toward down payment and closing costs, as a grant or a second lien forgiven after three years if you do not sell or refinance. Credit, income, and price limits apply and vary by county.

For most of these programs, you apply through a participating lender, so ask early. And ask the lender to show you the numbers with and without assistance, since rates and fees can differ.

## Step 3: Choose your agent and know the paperwork

In Texas, you will see two documents early. The **Information About Brokerage Services** notice explains how brokerages can work with you; TREC publishes the [official IABS form](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/information-about-brokerage-services). And since January 1, 2026, Texas law requires an agent to have a written agreement with a residential buyer before showing a home, or before presenting an offer if no home is shown. TREC's [summary of the 2026 changes](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/what-changes-2026-about-buyertenant-representation-texas) notes the agreement must say that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable.

## Step 4: Decide where to start looking

The best neighborhoods for first-time buyers in Houston are the ones that fit your budget, your commute, and the life you actually live. As you narrow your list, weigh:

- **Which city the home is in.** Not every Houston-area address pays City of Houston taxes. That matters for HAP, which requires it, and for Harris County's program, which covers areas outside the city.
- **Total monthly cost.** Taxes, HOA dues, and insurance vary by area and can matter as much as price.
- **Flood risk, address by address.** Check the [Harris County Flood Education Mapping Tool](https://www.harriscountyfemt.org/). For the official flood map, use FEMA's [Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/home), and ask your lender or insurance agent for an official determination.

### Are the Heights, Montrose, or Memorial realistic for a first home?

Sometimes, yes. Close-in neighborhoods often cost more than areas farther out, so first-time buyers who land there may choose a smaller home, a townhome, an older house that needs work, or a longer search. For some, being close in is worth that trade.

Assistance can shape that decision. Some programs have purchase price limits, some cover only certain areas, and HAP requires the home to pay City of Houston taxes. Not every address people call "Memorial" sits inside the City of Houston, so check before you count on city help. ## Step 5: Make an offer and use your option period well

Your offer will usually be written on a TREC contract form. Two protections are worth knowing.

**The option period.** Buyers often pay the seller an agreed option fee for the unrestricted right to terminate for any reason during a negotiated number of days. TREC explains that this is a [negotiable contract term](https://www.trec.texas.gov/i%E2%80%99m-buying-house-do-i-have-have-pay-option-period-be-able-buy-house) that many buyers use for inspections and repair negotiations. Deliver the fee on time, as the contract requires, or you lose that right. TREC also notes there is no automatic cooling-off period once a seller accepts your offer.

**The seller's disclosure.** Under [Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.5.htm), most sellers of a single-family home must give you a written notice that asks about past flooding, flood insurance, floodplain location, and flood claims, among other things. Some sales are exempt, including a new home that has never been lived in. If you sign a contract without receiving the notice, you may terminate for any reason within seven days after you receive it.

## FAQ

### How do I buy my first house in Houston?

Start with your budget and a lender pre-approval, take a homebuyer education class, and check which assistance programs fit. Then choose an agent, narrow your areas, and tour. Once under contract, use your option period for inspections before closing.

### What programs help first-time buyers in Houston?

Check the City of Houston Homebuyer Assistance Program, Harris County's Down Payment Assistance Program, TDHCA's My First Texas Home, and TSAHC's Home Sweet Texas and Homes for Texas Heroes. Some are currently closed or limited, so confirm with each program or a participating lender.

### Is Houston down payment assistance available right now?

It depends. As of this writing, the City of Houston says HAP is not accepting new applications, and Harris County's program is limited to applicants affected by Hurricane Beryl and the derecho. TDHCA and TSAHC programs are offered through participating lenders statewide.

### What is the option period when buying a house in Texas?

It is a negotiated number of days, paid for with an option fee, when you can end the contract for any reason by giving the seller written notice. Most buyers use it for inspections and repair negotiations.
