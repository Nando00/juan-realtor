Selling a house can feel like a long list of decisions you only make once or twice in your life. When do you list? What will it actually cost? What do you have to tell buyers? And do you even need an agent, or should you take one of those cash offers that keep landing in your mailbox?

This guide walks through how selling a house in Houston usually works, from the first walk-through to the day you hand over the keys, so you know what to expect and what to ask.

## The selling timeline, step by step

Every sale is different, but most follow the same basic path.

### 1. Get clear on your numbers and your move

Before you think about photos or paint, find your loan payoff, think about where you are going next, and decide whether you need to sell before you buy. That shapes how flexible you can be on price and closing date.

### 2. Price against what actually sold

Your list price should come from homes like yours that recently closed nearby, adjusted for condition, size, lot, and updates. Asking prices on homes still sitting on the market tell you what other sellers hope for, not what buyers are paying. If you want to see where the wider Houston market stands right now, our post on [selling your Houston home when supply is high](/blog/houston-selling-high-inventory-market) breaks down the latest Houston Association of Realtors figures.

### 3. Prepare the house

You do not need a renovation. You do need to remove the reasons a buyer might walk away or ask for a big credit: deferred maintenance, drainage problems, clutter that hides the rooms. Fresh paint, good light, a clean yard, and a few repairs usually go further than a big project.

### 4. List and show

Strong photos, an accurate description, and easy showings matter more than most sellers expect. Buyers compare your home with every listing in their price range before they step inside.

### 5. Negotiate and go under contract

Once you accept an offer, the contract sets the dates that run the rest of the deal: earnest money, the option period, financing, the appraisal, and closing.

### 6. Inspections, appraisal, and closing

The buyer inspects, the lender orders an appraisal if there is a loan, the title company clears title, and everyone signs at closing.

## What it really costs to sell a house in Houston

There is no single percentage. Your total depends on your loan, your contract, and what you negotiate. Here are the main pieces.

### Real estate commissions

Commissions are negotiable and not set by law. Since the 2024 national rule changes, how the buyer's agent gets paid is spelled out more clearly, and sellers decide whether to offer to cover any of it. Our guide to [whether you need a buyer's agent in Houston](/blog/do-you-need-a-buyers-agent-houston) explains how buyer agreements and fees work now.

### Title insurance and escrow fees

In Texas, title insurance premiums are set by the state. The Texas Department of Insurance [title insurance FAQ](https://tdi.texas.gov/title/titlefaqs.html) says every title company charges the same premium for a policy, based on the sale price, and that the buyer and seller can negotiate who pays it. Escrow fees and other closing charges, on the other hand, can differ between title companies, so they are worth comparing.

### Your loan payoff and prorated taxes

Your remaining mortgage balance and interest through the payoff date come out of your proceeds. Property taxes are typically prorated, so you pay your share of the current year up to closing. In Houston that can be a bigger number than sellers expect, so ask for an estimate early.

### HOA documents and fees

If your home has a property owners' association, the buyer will usually need a resale certificate. Under [Section 207.003 of the Texas Property Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.207.htm), the association must deliver it, along with the restrictions, bylaws, and rules, within 10 business days of a proper request. Expect a fee, and request it early.

### Repairs, credits, and concessions

Buyers often ask for repairs or a credit after the inspection, or help with their closing costs. None of it is required, but leave room for it in your bottom line.

### Taxes on the gain

Many homeowners owe no federal tax on the profit from selling their main home. According to the IRS [Topic No. 701, Sale of Your Home](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc701), you may be able to exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or up to $500,000 if you file jointly with your spouse, if you generally owned and lived in the home for at least two of the five years before the sale. There are exceptions, so check with a tax professional.

## The disclosures Texas sellers owe buyers

Disclosure sounds scarier than it is: tell buyers what you know, in writing, on time.

### The Seller's Disclosure Notice

[Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.5.htm) requires most sellers of a single-family home to give the buyer a written Seller's Disclosure Notice. It covers what you are aware of about the home's condition, including the roof, foundation, drainage, water damage, past repairs, and work done without permits.

The notice must be delivered on or before the contract's effective date. If it is not, the buyer may terminate for any reason within seven days after receiving it, so fill it out before you list. Some transfers, such as foreclosure sales and certain estate sales, are exempt.

### Flooding questions

Houston buyers read the flood section closely. The notice asks whether you are aware of past flooding, previous water penetration into a structure from a natural flood event, flood insurance coverage, and whether the property is wholly or partly in a 100-year or 500-year floodplain, a floodway, a flood pool, or a reservoir. Answer what you know and keep records of any repairs or mitigation work.

### Homes built before 1978

If your home was built before 1978, federal rules add a lead-based paint disclosure. The [EPA's disclosure rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-based-paint-disclosure-rule-section-1018-title-x) requires most sellers to disclose any known lead-based paint or hazards, share available reports, and give buyers the EPA pamphlet *Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home*. Buyers also get a 10-day opportunity to test for lead unless they waive it in writing or both sides agree to a different period.

If anything about your disclosures feels like it could carry legal risk, a real estate attorney is the right person to ask.

## Your options: an agent, FSBO, or a cash offer

### Listing with an agent

A listing agent handles pricing, marketing, showings, negotiation, and the steps to closing. The question is whether they add more than they cost, so ask how they would price and market your home and how often they will update you. Our post on [how to choose a realtor in Houston](/blog/how-to-choose-a-realtor-in-houston) has a list of interview questions you can use.

### Selling it yourself (FSBO)

Selling on your own can save a listing commission, but you own the pricing, photos, showings, contract, and deadlines. It works best when you already have a buyer and know the paperwork.

### Cash offers and instant buyers

Cash buyers and instant-offer companies trade price for speed and convenience. That can make sense if you need certainty or the house needs major work. Before you accept, compare their net after fees and repair deductions with what a listing could realistically bring.

## What to expect once you are under contract

Most Houston resales use the Texas Real Estate Commission's [One to Four Family Residential Contract](https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/20-19_3.pdf). Under it, the buyer usually pays an option fee for the right to terminate for any reason during the option period. That window is when inspections happen and repair requests come in. After it ends, the buyer's ability to walk away depends on the other terms of the contract, such as financing and the appraisal.

Quick answers to inspection questions and easy access for the appraiser keep a deal on track.

## FAQ

### How do I sell my house in Houston?

Know your payoff and goals, price against homes that actually sold nearby, fix what buyers will notice, list with strong photos, then work through the option period, appraisal, and closing.

### How much does it cost to sell a house in Houston?

It depends on the commission you negotiate, who pays the title policy, escrow fees, your loan payoff, prorated property taxes, HOA fees, and any repairs or credits you agree to. Ask for a written net sheet before you list.

### Do I have to disclose flooding when I sell a house in Texas?

Yes. The Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice asks whether you are aware of past flooding, water entering the home from a natural flood event, flood insurance, and whether the property is in a mapped floodplain.

### Should I sell my house myself or use a realtor in Houston?

Selling yourself can save a commission if you have the time and know the process. An agent makes more sense when you want help with pricing, marketing, negotiation, and keeping the contract on schedule.
