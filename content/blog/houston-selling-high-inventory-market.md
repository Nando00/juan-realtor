If you are thinking about selling your Houston home this winter, the headlines can feel loud. More homes are for sale. Fewer are selling than last year. Friends may tell you to wait until spring, or to drop your price now.

The useful question is more specific: what do the latest numbers actually say, and what should you change about price, timing, and presentation?

The [Houston Association of Realtors' October 2026 Housing Market Update](https://www.har.com/content/department/mls), released November 11, is the most recent official picture of the Greater Houston market. Use it to be precise, not to panic or to list at last year's number and hope.

## What October's numbers actually say

HAR's October update reported:

- **40,253 single-family homes for sale** across the Houston area, up 6.8% from last October.
- **A 5.5-month supply**, unchanged from September and the highest since 2012, which is how HAR describes a 14-year high. Nationwide, HAR cited a 4.8-month supply from the National Association of Realtors.
- **6,348 single-family sales**, down 14.9% from 7,458 a year earlier, the fewest for any October in the past 10 years.
- **Pending sales down 17.6%** to 6,445.
- **A median price of $320,000**, down 3.0% from a year earlier and the lowest since January 2024. The average price held steady at $421,100.
- **An average of 59 days on the market**, unchanged from last October.

HAR's [Monthly Activity Snapshot](https://www.harconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/har_MAS-26-10.pdf) for October adds that new single-family listings fell 8.2% from last October, to 14,069, and showings were down 5.2%.

HAR Chair Theresa Hill put it this way: "We typically see activity ease as the year winds down, but buyers who are still looking may have more time to find a home that fits their needs and budget."

These are metro-wide figures. Conditions in the Heights, Montrose, Memorial, or your price range can look different, so ask for recent solds from your neighborhood before you decide. Despite October's decline, HAR said single-family sales through the first 10 months of 2026 were still within 2% of last year's pace. The market has slowed, not stopped.

## Price to today's buyers, not last year's number

Price is usually the first thing that has to change in a market with more choices. Start with the homes near you that actually sold recently, not asking prices still on the market, and not what a similar house might have brought a year or two ago. HAR's October median of $320,000 is a metro figure, not your number.

The slowdown is not the same in every price range. According to HAR, October single-family sales changed year over year as follows:

| Price range | October sales | Change from last year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $1 to $99,999 | 97 | up 2.1% |
| $100,000 to $149,999 | 191 | down 1.0% |
| $150,000 to $249,999 | 1,339 | down 9.4% |
| $250,000 to $499,999 | 3,368 | down 16.8% |
| $500,000 to $999,999 | 1,061 | down 16.9% |
| $1 million and above | 291 | down 11.6% |

Most Houston sales still happened between $250,000 and $499,999, and that band, along with homes from $500,000 to $999,999, saw the steepest declines. Pricing has to be especially tight there. Existing homes, not including new construction, also slowed: HAR reported those sales down 10.2%, with a median of $315,000, down 4.5%.

### Why listing high and cutting later often backfires

It is tempting to test a high number "just to see." With more than 40,000 single-family homes for sale, many buyers will skip a house that looks expensive next to its neighbors and may not come back later. A small cut after weeks on the market often fails to reset attention. A clear, well-supported price on day one usually does more.

If the home is already listed and showings are thin, one thoughtful adjustment, explained with recent sales, is often better than several tiny drops. Price is not the only lever. Some sellers keep the list price and offer a closing-cost credit or a rate buydown instead, which can ease the monthly payment for a buyer facing the [7.33% average mortgage rate](https://www.har.com/content/department/mls) HAR reported for October, 108 basis points higher than a year earlier.

## Should you list this winter, or wait?

It depends on your timeline, not on a headline. Winter is often quieter. HAR's chair said activity typically eases as the year winds down, and October already showed fewer sales, fewer pending contracts, and fewer showings than a year earlier. If you can wait, run the numbers for your neighborhood first.

Waiting is not automatically safer. Inventory is already at its highest level since 2012, and spring could bring even more competition. Buyers still looking now may have more time, and fewer brand-new listings to compare: October's new listings were down 8.2% from last year.

If you need to sell because of a job change, a move, or a second payment, a well-priced winter listing can still work. The buyers who remain tend to be purposeful, including [first-time buyers](/blog/first-time-home-buyer-houston) who have been waiting for more options.

A good decision starts with a local snapshot:

- How many homes like yours are active in your area and price range?
- What did the recent solds actually close for, and how long did they take?
- What would waiting until spring cost you, and what would you use that time to improve?

## If you list, compete on more than the number

### Presentation

Photos are still the first showing. They should be bright, level, and honest about the light and layout, and the first photo has to earn the click. A floor plan or short video helps buyers comparing several homes at once. Inside, you do not need a renovation: fresh paint where it is scuffed, less furniture, clear counters, and a tidy entry help buyers picture themselves in the space.

### Access and condition

A home that is hard to show gets skipped when buyers have many other options. Reasonable showing hours and clear instructions matter more in a slower market, not less.

Be ready for condition questions, too. Under [Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.5.htm), sellers of most single-family homes must give buyers a written Seller's Disclosure Notice, which asks about defects, repairs, flood insurance, past flooding, and floodplain location. In Houston, those flood questions come up early. Receipts or a pre-listing inspection can help a nervous buyer feel confident.

If you are selling in the Heights, buyers may also ask about historic-district rules and deed restrictions. Our [Houston Heights realtor guide](/blog/houston-heights-realtor) covers what to check.

### A written plan, and the right partner

Before you go live, agree on a price backed by recent sales, new photos, a short prep list, easy showing access, and a date to review feedback. ## FAQ

### Is now a bad time to sell a house in Houston?

Not automatically. HAR's October 2026 report shows more competition and fewer sales than last year, with a 5.5-month supply, the highest since 2012. Homes are still selling. Sellers who price to recent sales and make the home easy to see usually do better.

### How should I price my Houston home in this market?

Start with recent closed sales of similar homes nearby, then adjust for condition, updates, and what else is for sale. The $320,000 metro median is a backdrop, not your number. Listing high to "test the market" is riskier when buyers can compare so many other homes.

### Should I wait until spring to list my Houston home?

It depends on your timeline. Activity often eases toward year-end, and October already looked slower than last year. If you can wait, use the time to prep. If you need to move, a well-priced winter listing can still reach serious buyers, and October brought fewer new listings than a year earlier.

### What if I already listed and need to cut the price?

Look at showings and recent solds first. A small cut after the home has gone stale often does not reset attention. One well-supported adjustment, or a credit or rate buydown, is usually more effective than several tiny drops.

### How long are Houston homes taking to sell?

HAR reported an average of 59 days on the market in October, the same as last October. That is a regional average. A well-priced home can move faster; a home priced above nearby options can sit much longer.
